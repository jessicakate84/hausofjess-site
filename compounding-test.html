The Compounding Test

Six pillars. One result: the experience.

Great companies don't grow by accident. They compound. Score your business from 1 (fragile) to 5 (indispensable) on each of the six pillars below. Together they produce the score that matters most: the experience your customers, partners and team actually have. Be honest. Nobody's watching.

The scale

1 · Fragile: It barely exists, or it's holding you back.
2 · Reactive: It works when someone pushes it. Inconsistent.
3 · Functional: Solid and dependable, but it doesn't set you apart.
4 · Strong: A real advantage that customers and partners notice.
5 · Indispensable: They'd feel it immediately if it disappeared.

People

Your people carry the experience to every customer. This isn't only about how talented they are. It's whether the team is built to perform without burning out.

Before you score, consider
  • Do you have enough people to run the business well, or is everyone stretched thin?
  • Is the workload balanced, or do a few people carry most of the weight?
  • Are they hungry and driven, or just getting through the week?
  • Are they paid and rewarded in a way that keeps your best people?
  • Do they have the training and tools to do the job well?

The test: Would your best clients ask where your people went?

Brand

Your brand is the reputation that walks into the room before you do. It isn't your logo. It's what people say about you when you're not there.

Before you score, consider
  • Do prospects already know who you are before you call?
  • Can your team and partners explain what makes you different in one sentence?
  • Do customers refer you without being asked?
  • Do you win on value, or does every deal come down to price?
  • Does what you promise match what customers actually experience?

The test: Would partners still choose you if your logo came off the product?

Strategy

Strategy is the set of choices that tells everyone where to focus. Good strategy says no as clearly as it says yes.

Before you score, consider
  • Do you know which markets, customers and deals you're not going after?
  • Is everyone, from leadership to sales, working toward the same priorities?
  • Do you know where competitors are weak, and how you'll win there?
  • Is the plan reviewed and adjusted regularly, or written once and forgotten?
  • Are new opportunities judged against the strategy, or chased because they showed up?

The test: Would your team describe the same strategy without a slide?

Operations

Operations is how reliably you deliver what you sell. It's what keeps the experience consistent as volume grows.

Before you score, consider
  • Do things run the same way every time, or depend on who's working that day?
  • Are your processes written down, or do they live in people's heads?
  • Do you see problems coming, or hear about them from the customer?
  • Do sales, delivery and support hand work to each other cleanly?
  • Are you measuring the few things that actually predict a good experience?

The test: Could you double volume without breaking the experience?

Financial Flexibility

Financial flexibility is the room to make smart moves. Revenue matters, but so does whether the money gives you options.

Before you score, consider
  • How much of next year's revenue is recurring or already contracted?
  • Are your margins healthy, or quietly slipping through discounts and rebates?
  • Is cash flow predictable enough to plan around?
  • Do you have more than one meaningful revenue stream?
  • Could you fund a new hire, market or product without putting the business at risk?

The test: Could you fund the next big opportunity without a crisis?

Leadership

Leadership sets the pace for everything else. The question isn't how hard your leaders work. It's whether they've built a business that doesn't depend on them for everything.

Before you score, consider
  • Do leaders spend their time building the business, or putting out fires?
  • Are decisions made quickly, and at the right level?
  • Is there a clear, regular rhythm for reviewing progress?
  • Do people know what's expected of them and how they're measured?
  • Is the leadership team developing the next layer of leaders?

The test: Would the flywheel keep turning if leadership stepped away for a month?

The result

Experience

You don't score this one. Experience is what the six pillars add up to: the way it actually feels to buy from you, partner with you and work with you.
